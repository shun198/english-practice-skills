## English Summary

### Topics We Covered

* How to conduct technical discussions naturally in English.
* How to explain engineering decisions clearly in a meeting.
* The importance of separating the **problem, proposed solution, trade-offs, and rationale** when discussing architecture or implementation choices.
* How to make technical explanations sound more concise and collaborative rather than like a prepared presentation.

### Key Questions I Asked

* Questions about the reasoning behind technical decisions.
* Questions about alternatives and trade-offs.
* Clarifying questions to understand assumptions, constraints, and expected behavior.
* Follow-up questions similar to what a senior engineer might ask during a design or architecture discussion.

### Specific Feedback On My English

* Your technical meaning was generally understandable, but some expressions could be made more natural by using shorter, more direct sentence structures.
* When explaining a decision, explicitly connecting the **reason** to the **decision** makes your argument easier to follow.
* In engineering discussions, phrases such as **“The main reason is…”**, **“The trade-off here is…”**, and **“My concern is…”** help organize your explanation naturally.
* When you are uncertain, it is often better to state the assumption explicitly rather than making the statement sound absolute.

### Example Questions Or Phrases For Future Use

* “What was the main reason behind this decision?”
* “What alternatives did you consider?”
* “What trade-offs are we making here?”
* “What happens if this assumption turns out to be wrong?”
* “My understanding is that … Is that correct?”
* “One concern I have is …”
* “The main reason I prefer this approach is …”
* “From an implementation perspective, …”
* “I think this works, but I’m not sure it scales well if …”
* “Could you walk me through how this would work in practice?”
